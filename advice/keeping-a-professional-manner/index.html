Keeping a Professional Manner

I have been working on a project that involves four PIs, where I am the only RA and the only undergraduate. During the first meetings I was so afraid of saying something wrong, and of them seeing me differently afterwards. I have gained a lot of confidence since then, but somehow I still hold that feeling often.

I have also learned it the hard way, through my own mistakes: confusing ideas, not grasping the research material well enough, not finishing a report or sending the meeting recap on time. Those lessons have become a lot of the qualities and responsibilities I now hold myself to, and I hope these tips are useful for you.

  1. Always be on time with reports, and with meeting recaps. It is good to send a recap right after a meeting, so you do not forget it later.
  2. Keep communication clear and transparent. Do not assume other people have the same thoughts as you. When you raise something, in person and especially over email, include the context and what it is you want to bring up. Even after a meeting, people leave with contradicting ideas about what they and you will be doing, which is exactly why a recap is different. Be as clear and detailed as you can, and ask others for clarification when you do not understand them either.
  3. Keep everything well documented, including data, scripts and other documents. Having a backup that is up to date matters in case of data loss or corruption, and your data, scripts and results should be reproducible.
  4. You do not always have to be serious. You can talk about daily life and joke with your PIs and colleagues; just keep it professional when it comes to the work: explaining concepts, raising problems. A relaxed environment makes everyone comfortable.

More about the work I do: I am very fortunate to be the only RA on the project, and to be advised directly by PIs who have come through top institutions and well-known companies. I am happier still that they have said they want to train me to be independent and autonomous for the future. That has surely shaped the way I work.
